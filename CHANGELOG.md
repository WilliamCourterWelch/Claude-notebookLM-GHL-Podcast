# Changelog

All notable changes to globalhighlevel.com's static-site build are documented here.

## [0.3.17.0] - 2026-08-30
### Changed
- **The pricing guide now uses the plan name HighLevel actually uses.** The $497 tier was called "SaaS Pro" in 17 places across the page, its meta description and its FAQ structured data. HighLevel's official name is **Agency Pro** (gohighlevel.com/pricing; the billing guide calls SaaS Mode a *feature* of that plan, not a plan name). The page now leads with Agency Pro and keeps SaaS Pro as the common name, because Bing shows searchers using both: 15 impressions on this page's agency-pro queries, 10 of them spelling it "saas pro". A first pass renamed all 17 and dropped SaaS Pro to a single mention — adversarial review caught that as a coverage regression on the term this page's own searchers use more, and it was restored to 7.
- **Two new sections answering what people actually search.** "What the Agency Wallet Actually Pays For" documents the six common wallet-funded services, premium workflow actions and webhooks at $0.01 per execution, and the fact that phone and SMS follow Twilio's per-country rates so no single number exists. "The Main Paid Add-Ons, With Prices" is a 14-row table of HighLevel's published rates, from WhatsApp at $10/month to Advanced Account Setup at $1,000 one-time, plus the AI token rates and the Summer of AI promotion ending 1 September 2026. 3,212 → 4,434 words.
- **Four new FAQs**, added to the visible page *and* the hand-written FAQPage JSON-LD: setup fees and deposits, the difference between the three plans (covering the "packages" and "tiers" phrasings people search), premium action cost, and what currency you are billed in for euro, rupee and African-market buyers.

### Fixed
- **The page overstated the cost of email by roughly 15x.** It said "~$0.01 per email" in two places, including inside the FAQ structured data search engines read. HighLevel publishes **$0.675 per 1,000 emails**, the same on every plan — about seven hundredths of a cent each. Pre-existing, but this release re-verified the page's rates against official docs, so shipping the old number would have re-certified it. Corrected everywhere.

### Added
- **A gate that stops FAQ structured data drifting from the visible FAQ** (`scripts/test_faq_schema_sync.py`, 3 tests, suite 179 → 182). 159 posts carry hand-written `FAQPage` JSON-LD; search engines read that, not the prose, so a Q&A added to one side and not the other ships a page that contradicts itself with nothing rendering wrong. All 159 are currently in sync and there is deliberately **no allowlist**.

### For contributors
- **The gate's first draft was wrong in the way it was built, not the way it was written.** It hand-rolled an English-only `<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>` matcher, which silently skipped 42 of the 159 posts (Spanish, Arabic and "Common Questions" headings) and reported 3 false drifts by grabbing `<h3>`s from outside the FAQ section. Those 3 "pre-existing drifted posts" went into an allowlist that would have enshrined a detector bug as corpus debt. `scripts/fix_faq_schema.py` already had a heading pattern **enumerated from the corpus** and section-scoped extraction. Importing it took scope 117 → 159 and drift 3 → **0**. Search the repo before writing an extractor.
- **An exceptions list that only checks its entries are still broken is a mute button, not a ratchet.** The draft's `KNOWN_DRIFT` verified its slugs were still drifted — which does nothing to stop someone adding a newly drifted slug to silence a failure. Deleted rather than reinforced, once the real drift count turned out to be zero.
- **Adversarial review found 11 issues and two rounds were needed.** Round 1: the false email rate; "Every Paid Add-On" claiming a completeness the table does not have; "lists exactly six things" where the source says "common ... include"; WordPress hosting flattened to $10 when it is $10-$497 by plan; "also sold as SaaS Pro" implying an official sales name; AI metering stated without the subscription carve-out; a dated claim that would read false on 2 September. Round 2 caught that the intro still said "every published add-on" three paragraphs above the correction that removed it, and that the new test file was still untracked and therefore not actually in the diff.
- **Measured, not assumed: this is not expected to move the head terms.** `go high level pricing` (49 impressions, position 7.4) and `ghl pricing` (29, position 7.7) were already fully covered in the body before this change. Of the page's 411 Bing impressions, 58% sit on queries the page already answered, 34% on queries with real gaps (77 impressions of those at position 5 or better), and 8% are assistant-style noise. This targets the gaps. On-page is saturated for the head terms, which now rests on measurement rather than assumption.

## [0.3.16.1] - 2026-08-29
### Changed
- **The money page stops turning away the searchers it ranks for.** `/blog/gohighlevel-free-trial-30-days-extended/` is the site's biggest underperformer: **1,091 Bing impressions — a tenth of everything the site earns — at position 5.0 converting at 0.37%**, against 2.70% across the 250 ranked pages and 1.57% site-wide. Bing's top queries are overwhelmingly trial, discount and promo intent, and 12 discount-intent queries carrying 134 impressions currently earn **zero** clicks, several of them from position 1. The snippet ended "No fake coupon codes", which dismisses the exact person searching. It now reads *"Looking for a GoHighLevel coupon? Real savings include 30 days instead of 14 and ~17% off annual billing. $0 to start — just a ~$1 card-verification hold."* Same honesty, opposite posture: it answers the question instead of refusing it, and hands over a real number the page delivers.
- **The title is deliberately untouched.** "Get 30 Days Free, Not 14 — GoHighLevel Trial 2026" is 49 characters, already inside budget, and is the hook earning position 5.0. The repulsion was in the description; changing the title would risk the ranking for no identified gain.

### For contributors
- **Three adversarial rounds on 154 characters, and each round caught a real overclaim.** Round 1: "beats any coupon code" — false, because the page itself documents seasonal promotions that can beat 16 extra trial days. Round 2: "The real deal is 30 days instead of 14" — still implied exclusivity, and self-contradicted, since the same sentence names annual billing as a second deal. Round 3: shippable. The fix was the word **"include"**, which presents two savings routes without claiming they are the complete set. Copy this short is not cheap to get right; the failure mode was the same each time, an absolute claim the page cannot support.
- **No new page was created, and that was the finding.** The plan going in was to build an English discount page for the 134 impressions of unserved discount queries. Two checks killed it. First, the money page **already** covers discount intent in five sections ("The Truth About GoHighLevel Promo Codes", "Annual Billing Savings (~17% Off)", "Seasonal Promotions", "Why Coupon Aggregator Sites Are Wasting Your Time", "The Best Deal Available Right Now") — the earlier search had looked at slugs and titles, not bodies. Second, Spanish had already run the experiment: its standalone `Código Promocional GoHighLevel 2026` page does not appear among the 250 pages Bing's `GetPageStats` returns. **Correction 2026-08-30: this does NOT mean it earned zero impressions.** 250 is a hard cap in the Bing API, not a threshold — those 250 pages account for only 5,155 of the site's 11,118 impressions, so more than half of all page impressions sit outside the list entirely. The page's traffic is unknown, not zero. The content existed; only the snippet was hiding it.
- **Gates are now recorded in the evidence ledger** (`gstack-evidence`), new in gstack 1.72.0.0. Every pytest, build and verify run on this branch is logged with its exit code, so Step 16 can prove freshness rather than trusting a claim.

### Documentation
- **The repo had nothing at all on meta descriptions.** `globalhighlevel-site/CLAUDE.md` documented the 60-character title budget in detail and never once mentioned descriptions. It now carries the rule this release learned: **a page can rank well and convert terribly because its snippet turns away the searcher, so read the description against the queries the page ranks for before blaming the content or the ranking.** Plus the 150-160 character budget, the `~$1` disclosure requirement that applies inside descriptions, and the two verification habits that stopped a page from being written — search page BODIES not slugs, and check whether another language already ran the format.
- **Four numbers in this entry were corrected against a fresh pull, and one of them could not be reproduced at all.** The "159 impressions of discount-intent queries" figure did not fall out of any stated keyword net; the reproducible number is **12 queries carrying 134 impressions**, all at zero clicks (net: coupon|discount|promo|code over the 300 top queries, 2026-08-29 pull). The title is **49** characters, not 51. The Spanish page is absent from the **250** ranked pages, not 300 — 300 was the query count. And "2.70% site average" was the average across ranked pages; site-wide is **1.57%**. A number in a shipped changelog that nobody can re-derive is a claim, not evidence.
- **A gate's docstrings contradicted its own code.** `scripts/test_zero_cost_claims_disclosed.py` said in two places that a zero-cost claim may be disclosed by "the ~$1 hold **or** the truthful cancel line". Its `DISCLOSURE` pattern accepts only the card-verification hold — a Codex round on 2026-08-20 deliberately removed the cancel line, because "cancel any time" speaks to the cancellation policy and not to what the card is charged at signup. The code was right and the prose was six days stale. Corrected; the 4 tests still pass.

### Changed

If they reach 5.5% CTR that is roughly **+35 clicks per feed window, about a 23% lift on the site's 152 total Bing clicks**. Each new title is 46-58 characters, so none are truncated, and two of them drop a bare `&` that was shipping unescaped into `<title>`.
- **The ratchet moves for the first time: 590 → 580.** `verify.py` Check 7 now carries its own history in a comment, so the next person can see the budget shrinking rather than guessing at the number.

### For contributors
- **Google said don't do this work. Bing said do it.** The first pass at prioritising these titles used Search Console and concluded the opposite — that rewriting them was not worth it. That read was correct *for Google*, where the site is close to dead: 203 of 927 URLs known, 1,524 impressions and **5 clicks** in 90 days, and the overlong pages earning zero clicks from 499 impressions. Bill redirected to Bing, where the same pages carry 1,278 impressions and 45 clicks at strong positions. **The recommendation flipped entirely on which search engine the data came from.** A percentage of pages is not a percentage of traffic, and neither is meaningful until you name the surface.
- **Titles are page headings, not just snippets.** A post's `title` drives the visible `<h1 class="post-title">` as well as `<title>`, so all ten were written to read as headings and were checked against each page's own `<h2>` list before being written. Every specific claim traces to something the page actually contains: "5 Steps" to five numbered sections, "10 per Location, All Plans" to the October 2025 plan-limit change, "Which Plan Adds Markup" to the section that names Agency Pro.
- **Adversarial review caught an understated cost and a term worth 14 redirects.** The HIPAA title first read "The $297 Add-On", which reads as one-time for a charge that is $297 **per month** — the kind of understatement this project has been repeatedly wrong about in the other direction. And the LATAM title first dropped "Herramientas Locales", not realising that **14 URLs 301 into that page** from `vs alternativas` and `vs herramientas locales` sources; dropping the term would have discarded the topical match for an entire consolidated cluster.
- **The first attempt rewrote more than it meant to.** Applying the titles by loading and re-dumping each JSON re-escaped one file's whole `html_content` blob. Semantically identical, verified field by field, but this repo's doctrine is that post JSON stays byte-faithful. Redone as line-level replacements that match each file's existing escaping convention, leaving a diff of exactly ten lines across ten files.
- **Assessed and deliberately not fixed:** two of these pages still lead with "Qué es GoHighLevel" and still compete. Codex asked for a revision; the only title-level fix would be dropping the head term one of them ranks on at position 6.2, which Codex had itself flagged as the top ranking risk a round earlier. Both *old* titles carried the same head term, so this release does not worsen the overlap and modestly improves it. The real fix is consolidating the cluster, which means removing a live page and remains the owner's call.

## [0.3.15.0] - 2026-08-25
### Fixed
- **95% of the site had a title too long for Google to show.** Measured across all 980 built pages: median title 83 characters, and only 1% inside the 50-60 band that Caleb Ulku's non-local SEO canon prescribes (`lessons/12-non-local-seo/click-through-rate-ctr-analysis.md`), which is also about where Google truncates. The cause was mechanical: `" | Global High Level"` was appended unconditionally at ten call sites plus a separate page template, putting 20 characters of publisher name on 973 of 980 pages. A new `compose_title()` appends the brand **only when the result still fits**. Median drops to 63, the in-band share goes 1% → 33%, and the truncated share goes 95% → 60%. No visible `<h1>` changes — the suffix only ever existed in `<title>`.
- **The brand still appears where the page has nothing else to say.** It survives on any title of 40 characters or fewer — 44 indexable pages: the blog pagination (`Page 2` alone tells a searcher nothing), the category pages, two short posts, and the `/es/` and `/ar/` hubs. The `404` page carries it too, making 45 in the built tree, but it is `noindex` and sits outside the gate.

### Added
- **A ratchet and two invariants, so this cannot quietly come back.** `verify.py` Check 7 counts titles over 60 characters and fails if the number grows past 590. It is deliberately a ratchet and not a clean gate: 590 pages are still too long on their own words, and a hard fail would block every build until that copy work is done. Alongside it run two hard invariants — no page may keep the brand while overlong, and no page may have room for the brand and lack it. Either condition proves a title was composed outside `compose_title`. Both were mutation-tested, and both caught real bypasses during this release.
- **6 tests** on the composer (suite 173 → 179): both edges, the exact boundary, the guarantee that it never pushes a title over the limit itself, the idempotence guard, and that the 60-character budget has not drifted from canon.

### For contributors
- **Adversarial review took two rounds, and the first round caught a false claim rather than a bug.** The code comment and test docstring both stated the brand survives "on all `/page/N/` pagination". It does not — it survives on any title of 40 characters or fewer, which is 44 pages, and **16 of them are not pagination**. The claim came from reading the shortest few entries of a sorted list and generalising. That is the same shape as the count-over-collection overclaims from 2026-08-24, moved from user-facing copy into code comments, and it is the reason both files now carry an explicit "measure it, do not restate it from memory" note.
- **Three defects the gates would not have caught on their own**, all found in review: `compose_title` double-appended the brand on an already-branded title *short enough to fit* (the long-fixture test masked it, because the second suffix gets dropped for length anyway); a **second** page template at `build.py:1935` still hardcoded the suffix; and the category-pillar path routed only its fallback through the composer, so a future short pillar title would have silently lost its brand.
- **A limit worth stating: the 404 page is exempt.** Its title is now composed properly, but Check 7 skips `noindex` pages — a page excluded from search has no SERP title to budget, and without the exemption a future short `noindex` page (a `/thanks/` titled "Thanks") would trip the invariant for nothing. The consequence is that reverting the 404 to a hardcoded suffix would **not** fail the build. That is consistency hygiene, not a guarded invariant, and the mutation test confirms it.
- **Known and deferred:** 590 pages still exceed 60 characters on their own words (median 68). Those titles *are* the visible `<h1>` — a post's `title` drives both — so shortening them is copy work, not a mechanical pass. Lower `TITLE_OVERLONG_BASELINE` in `verify.py` as they land.
- **Unresolved tension, flagged not fixed:** this gate measures **characters** while the v0.3.13.0 hub-title gate measures **UTF-8 bytes** (`/es/` is 59 characters but 61 bytes). Codex's read is that both are proxies and neither models what Google actually truncates on, which is pixel width. They do not contradict each other on any current page. Filed rather than reconciled.

## [0.3.11.0] - 2026-08-20
### Fixed
- **Readers who decide on a plan can now act on it without scrolling back.** Both pricing guides described all three tiers — Starter $97, Unlimited $297, SaaS Pro $497 — and the comparison table with no trial link anywhere among them. The English guide put one CTA at 9% depth (before the reader knows any price), then nothing until 40.1%; the Spanish guide went from 5.5% to 54.9%. That dead zone was 13,070 characters, 37% of the English article and 58% of the Spanish one, and it covered the exact moment someone decides "$97 works for me". Clarity puts average scroll depth at 38.7%, so the average reader left the English page just before the next CTA existed. The measured result over eight weeks: 20 pageviews, zero click events of any kind, zero affiliate clicks — while the free-trial post converted 6 of 23 sessions (26%) off five CTAs stacked in its first 14%. Four in-body CTAs were added to each guide, after each tier and under the comparison table. Largest remaining gap: 24.5% (EN) and 27.0% (ES), with three CTAs now landing at 13-22% depth where readers actually are.
- Each new CTA carries its own `utm_content` (`tier_starter`, `tier_unlimited`, `tier_saaspro`, `after_comparison`), so which tier actually converts is answerable from GA4 rather than inferred. The existing `ghl_click` listener already fires on any `fp_ref=` anchor, so no tracking changes were needed.

### Added
- **`scripts/test_zero_cost_claims_disclosed.py`** — a corpus gate for the *implicit* version of the false-trial claim. `test_trial_claims_residual.py` catches the explicit phrasing ("no credit card", "sin tarjeta"); it does not catch a bare "$0 to start" with no mention of the ~$1 card-verification hold, which promises the same thing to a buyer without ever using the words the older gate matches. The new gate requires a real hold disclosure within 200 characters of any zero-cost claim, reads `$` in all three HTML spellings (literal, `&#36;`, `&dollar;`) and whitespace including `&nbsp;`, and carries an `ALLOWLIST` escape hatch for honest quotation, mirroring its sibling. Across 909 posts it matches 25 claims in 12 files, all disclosed. Suite 148 → 152.

### For contributors
- **Two defects in this change were introduced by matching the wrong thing, and both were caught by adversarial review before they shipped.** The first: the four new CTAs were given `utm_source=blog&utm_medium=article`, copied from links already on the two pages being edited. Corpus-wide that variant is 44 uses (2.3%) while `utm_source=globalhighlevel&utm_medium=blog` is 1,859 (97%) and is what CLAUDE.md specifies — the pages being edited were themselves the outlier. Measure a convention across the corpus before copying it out of the file in front of you. The second: the new gate's disclosure set initially accepted "cancel any time" / "cancela cuando quieras" as satisfying the ~$1 hold requirement. It does not; a cancellation promise describes the cancellation policy, not what the card is charged at signup, so "$0 upfront, cancel any time" would have passed the gate while still implying no card is needed. Both are pinned by tests (`test_cancellation_promise_is_not_a_cost_disclosure`, `test_gate_catches_spelling_variants`).

## [0.1.0.0] - 2026-05-27
### Added
- **`topic` as the real category axis, separate from language (language × topic restructure).**
  Every post now carries a `topic` field (one of 8 canonical topics) independent of its
  `language`. `build.py` organizes the site by `topic` (via a new `post_topic()` helper with
  a `category` fallback), so the two axes are no longer tangled. 373 posts that were trapped
  under language-bucket "categories" (`GoHighLevel en Español` / `GoHighLevel India`) now
  surface under their real topic.
- **`verify.py` phase-gate harness.** Re-runnable audit with 4 checks: Check 0 (a post's
  `language` field must not contradict its slug markers — catches a bad migration write that
  the other checks, which trust the field, cannot), Check 1 (root `/category/` English-only),
  Check 2 (no orphaned posts), Check 3 (no dangling internal links, allowlist now empty).
- **`migrate_lang_topic.py` reviewable migration tool.** Proposal-first (writes a CSV for
  human review), idempotent `--apply` that reads the reviewed CSV, fail-loud validation
  (rejects bad language/topic, aborts on duplicate slugs), and a writer that preserves each
  file's existing encoding + newline so a 2-field change is a 2-line diff, not a reformat.

### Changed

  469 posts backfilled `language`). `category` kept for back-compat. Migration was AI-classified
  with the uncertain calls reviewed by hand.
- **Daily generators (5-blog, 6-india, 7-spanish, 9-arabic) stamp `topic` + `language`** on every
  new post, so the nightly pipeline no longer re-creates the language/topic tangle.
