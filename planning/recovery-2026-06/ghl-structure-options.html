Your idea: make the trial page the center — homepage feeds it, and instead of just bouncing people off to GoHighLevel, it also routes them into "Which are you?". Here's that head-to-head with the hub-funnel, honestly.

Pros

  • Pours the homepage's authority onto your single best-converting page — could push it up for "gohighlevel free trial / 30 day trial"
  • Stops the trial page being a dead-end that only bounces to the affiliate — it now keeps people on-site
  • Internal links match where the money actually is

Cons

  • Buries the hubs a level deeper (2 clicks from home, not 1) — they get less authority and rank weaker for "gohighlevel agency / for service business"
  • Re-mixes the silos — both topics now funnel through one non-topical conversion page, the chokepoint you're trying to avoid
  • Dilutes the money page's one job — adding "which are you?" links next to its trial CTA competes with the conversion (the exact thing we just fixed on the homepage)
  • One page becomes a single point of failure for the whole tree

Pros

  • You don't have to choose. The homepage has enough authority to make all three (2 hubs + trial page) strong level-1 pages at once
  • Hubs stay 1 click from home → maximum ranking power for the head terms
  • Clean silos — each hub owns its topic, no shared chokepoint
  • Trial page stays a focused converter: receives a direct strong link, sends to the affiliate, nothing competing

Cons

  • The trial page isn't the single "center" — authority is shared across 3 pages instead of stacked on 1
  • Needs the bottom "by topic" grid removed/repointed so the homepage isn't also scattering to spokes
The key thing your idea gets right — and how we keep it without the cost: you're right that the trial page shouldn't just dump people off-site. So in Option A we still add 2 small contextual links from the trial page to the hubs ("New to GoHighLevel? See what it does for service businesses / agencies") — but placed below its trial CTA, as a secondary path for people who aren't ready to convert. That captures your "keep them on-site, route to who-are-you" instinct without burying the hubs or diluting the CTA.
Why A beats B in one line: B makes one page strong by making the hubs weaker (2 clicks down). A makes the hubs and the trial page strong at the same time, because they're all direct children of your strongest page. You're not trading — you get both.
