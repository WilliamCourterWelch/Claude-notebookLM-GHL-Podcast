## Who should NOT use this

Start here, because the honest answer for a lot of businesses is "not yet."

**Skip it (for now) if:**

- **You're a solo operator with low call volume.** If you can return a voicemail in ten minutes, a free missed-call-text-back plus voicemail will lose you fewer jobs than an AI that occasionally talks over a caller. You don't need a CRM and an AI agent to answer five calls a day.
- **Every call is a high-stakes emergency.** Surveys consistently show people prefer a human on the phone. In [one 2025 survey of 1,011 US consumers](https://kinsta.com/blog/ai-vs-human-customer-service/) (commissioned by Kinsta, run by Propeller Insights), 93% said they'd rather reach a human and about half said they'd consider leaving a company over AI-only service. For a flooding basement or a dead furnace at 11pm, a caller who hits a robotic agent may just dial the next number — and you'll never know the job walked.
- **You serve a lot of non-English callers.** GoHighLevel users still report (as recently as early 2026) that the Voice AI struggles to spell names and emails outside English.
- **You won't set up human escalation.** An AI receptionist is only safe when it can hand a tricky call to a person. If you can't wire that up, don't put it on your main line.

**It fits when:** you already run on GoHighLevel (or want the whole suite), your call volume is high enough that calls are genuinely getting missed, the common calls are repetitive (hours, directions, simple booking), and you set up a clean "press 0 for a human" path.

## The real call limits (and a myth to ignore)

You may have read GoHighLevel's Voice AI is capped at "~100 calls a day." **That's wrong.** GoHighLevel's own documentation (as of July 2026) sets the real limits:

- Up to [1,000 outbound Voice AI calls per location per day](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000006598-voice-ai-outbound-calling), with calls above the limit scheduled for the next eligible day.
- An outbound pacing limit of **10 calls per minute** (one every six seconds) — a dialing-speed limit, not a cap on how many calls can happen at once.
- Outbound frequency caps per contact: **each number can be called once per day and up to 14 times in 14 days**, calls go out between 8am and 8pm in the contact's timezone, and only to same-country numbers.
- On the inbound side, a Voice AI number getting hit at [more than 200 calls per minute triggers a violation and blocks that call](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000005314-understanding-leadconnector-inbound-call-rate-limits) — a spam guardrail, not a daily ceiling.
- A caution worth knowing: **more than 5 rate-limit violations in an hour blocks all inbound calls account-wide** until you contact support.

For a normal local business, you will not hit the daily or per-minute caps. The one to respect is the violations guardrail if you're running aggressive outbound campaigns.

## Before you turn it on: the legal basics

This is not legal advice, and the rules depend on your state and how you use the AI. Talk to an attorney. But here's what a business owner should at least know in 2026:

- **Outbound AI calls.** The FCC ruled (February 2024) that AI-generated voices count as an "artificial voice" under the TCPA, so **making outbound calls with an AI voice can require the called person's prior express consent.** This is about *outbound* AI calling and robocalls. An AI receptionist *answering* calls that customers place to you is a different situation the ruling doesn't squarely cover — but if you use Voice AI to *dial out*, get advice first. (GoHighLevel runs its own [compliance checks on outbound Voice AI](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000006679-voice-ai-outbound-calling-compliance-checks), but their checks don't replace your obligations.)
- **Telling callers it's AI.** Some states have bot- or AI-disclosure rules. California's BOT Act and Utah's AI Policy Act are examples (Utah's was narrowed by 2025 amendments). Whether you have to disclose, and how, **depends on your state, your industry, and how the bot is used** — don't assume every call carries the same duty.
- **Recording calls.** Many states require all-party consent to record. If your AI receptionist records or transcribes, check your state's rule.
- **Texting.** If you pair the AI with SMS follow-up, US carriers require A2P 10DLC registration and proper consent for business texting.

The short version: this is powerful, and "just turn it on" is not responsible advice. Spend an hour with these before you go live.
